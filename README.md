<h1 align="center">Lectern</h1>

<p align="center"><b>Project one window, not your whole screen.</b><br>
Zoom, highlight and point things out while you present, from a small toolbar, without leaving your editor.</p>

<p align="center"><a href="https://github.com/Lord-Raven1/Lectern-Release/releases/latest"><b>⬇ Download the latest version</b></a></p>

---

Lectern is for anyone who presents from a laptop, especially code. It was made for lecture halls that project with Epson iProjection, but it works with any projector or projection software that gives Windows a second screen.

## Why

- **Privacy:** sharing your whole screen shows the room your notifications, tabs and everything else. Lectern shows only the window you pick.
- **Readability:** code is tiny on a projector, and wireless projection blurs it. Lectern zooms smoothly into exactly what you're talking about.
- **Pointing:** a normal mouse pointer gets lost on a big screen. Lectern shows a big ring the room can follow, plus highlights and notes.

## What you get

- **One window on the projector.** VS Code, a browser, a document or slides. Your laptop screen stays yours: check something in another app and the projector holds the last picture until you come back.
- **A floating toolbar** on your window (on your laptop only, never on the projector) with a button for everything.
- **Zoom:** click a spot, drag a box, or scroll. A box locks the view on that area; otherwise it gently follows your mouse.
- **Highlight lines:** a band previews under your mouse so you see the line before you click. Click line after line, or drag over a block.
- **Spotlight:** dim everything except what matters.
- **Notes:** short labels pointing at code. Drag them to move them.
- **Back in a moment and Freeze:** hide the screen, or hold the picture while you look something up.
- **Room view:** when zoomed, a dashed outline on your laptop shows exactly what the room sees.
- **Shortcuts for the essentials:** `Ctrl Alt A` zoom, `Ctrl Alt H` highlight, `Ctrl Alt F` follow the mouse, `Esc` to finish.

Highlights and notes are drawn on the projector only. Lectern never changes your files.

## Download

Get these from the [latest release](https://github.com/Lord-Raven1/Lectern-Release/releases/latest). None of them needs admin rights.

| File | Best for |
|---|---|
| `Lectern-Portable-<version>.exe` | **Work or campus laptops.** One file, nothing to install. Keep it on a USB stick or in Documents. |
| `Lectern-<version>-win.zip` | **If the portable one won't start** (some IT policies block programs that run from temporary folders). Unzip it anywhere and run `Lectern.exe`. |
| `Lectern-Setup-<version>.exe` | **Your own laptop.** Installs just for you, with a Start menu and desktop shortcut. |

Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit.

### "Windows protected your PC"

Lectern is free and isn't code-signed yet (a certificate costs money every year), so Windows SmartScreen may warn you the first time. Click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

For the zip: if Windows blocks `Lectern.exe` after unzipping, right-click the downloaded zip first, choose **Properties**, tick **Unblock**, then unzip it again.

If your organisation's IT has turned off "Run anyway", ask them to allow Lectern, or use your own laptop.

## How to use it

1. **Connect to the projector as a second screen.**
   - **Epson iProjection:** use its extended display mode.
   - **Other ways:** an HDMI or VGA cable, Miracast (`Win K`) or another projector app all work the same way.

   Use extend, not duplicate (mirror) mode. In mirror mode the room sees your laptop screen.
2. **Open Lectern**, pick the window to show, check the projector is selected, and press **Start showing**.
3. **Present.** Work in that window as normal and use the toolbar that appears on it.

**Keep your app on your own screen.** You extend to the projector so Lectern has a screen to draw on, but you don't put anything there yourself. Lectern mirrors your one window onto it, like PowerPoint's presenter view. If the window ends up on the projector screen, Lectern moves it back to yours.

No projector? Lectern shows the stage in an ordinary window instead. You can share that window in Teams or Zoom to get the same zoom and highlights online.

## Privacy

Lectern collects nothing: no accounts, analytics or tracking. The window you pick goes only to your own projector, and nothing is recorded or sent anywhere. Read the [terms of use and privacy](TERMS.md), which are also in the app.
